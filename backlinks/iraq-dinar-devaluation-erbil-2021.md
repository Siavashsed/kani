## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How did the Iraqi dinar devaluation in 2020-2021 affect prices and businesses in Erbil and the Kurdistan Region?

The short version: the move to roughly 1,450 dinars per dollar made imported goods in the Kurdistan Region about 20 percent more expensive almost overnight, and most of 2021 was spent working out who actually paid for it.

The Kurdistan Region imports a large share of its food, clothing, electronics and building materials, much of it from Turkey and Iran and priced in dollars. When the dinar weakened against the dollar, the local cost of those goods rose immediately, even though incomes were still in dinars.

The burden fell unevenly. Importers who had bought goods on dollar credit were hit hardest. In Erbil's Qaysari bazaar, one cloth importer we spoke with had purchased 40 rolls of Turkish fabric on credit in dollars, and the invoice he had budgeted for came due at a much higher dinar amount. He could not pass the full increase to customers, so he absorbed part of it and raised prices on the rest.

Households felt it in imported staples and household goods first. Locally produced items moved less, which pushed some shoppers toward local substitutes where they existed. Salaries, particularly in the public sector, did not rise to compensate, so the adjustment happened inside the shopping cart.

Small retailers faced a second problem: customers delayed purchases and bargained harder, which squeezed margins further.

Businesses that coped best shortened their credit terms, bought smaller and more often, diversified suppliers, and moved toward local sourcing where quality allowed.
