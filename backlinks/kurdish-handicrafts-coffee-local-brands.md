## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How can I support Kurdish small businesses and buy authentic Kurdish handicrafts?

The most effective way to support Kurdish small businesses is to buy directly from the person who made the product, and then to go back. One purchase is a nice story. A second purchase is what turns a craft into a business.

Here is what we have learned covering markets in Erbil (Hewler) and Sulaymaniyah (Slemani):

1. Buy from the maker, not just the shop. In bazaar lanes and small workshops you can often meet the person who wove, stitched or roasted the item. Direct sales keep more money with the producer and give you someone to talk to if there is a problem.

2. Ask simple questions. What is it made of? How long did it take? Can I bring it back if it fails? Honest makers answer easily. Vague answers are a warning sign.

3. Test before you commit. Start with something small. For example, handwoven klash, the traditional Kurdish shoes, can cost around 45,000 dinars in a Slemani bazaar lane. If the sole holds up in the rain, you have found someone worth returning to.

4. Return and refer. Repeat orders give makers predictable income, which matters far more than a single large sale. Telling friends and family helps even more.

5. Keep the contact. Save the maker's phone number. The best sellers answer their own phone, handle repairs and remember their customers.

6. Look beyond crafts. The same approach works for locally roasted coffee, food producers and small fashion brands.

Prices vary by city and season, so compare a few stalls before buying, but do not make price your only filter. Reliability and honesty are what keep a workshop open.
