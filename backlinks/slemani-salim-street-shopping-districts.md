## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Where do locals actually shop in Sulaymaniyah (Slemani), Iraqi Kurdistan?

Short answer: it depends on what you're buying, and the most famous street is often not the right place.

Salim Street is Slemani's showcase. It's the long commercial avenue with the brand storefronts, cafes and evening crowds, and it's a good place to walk, eat and browse. For most serious purchases, though, residents go elsewhere.

Phones and electronics: many locals compare prices in the old bazaar area behind the Grand Mosque. Sellers sit close together, so you can collect three or four quotes in a short walk.

Gold: also the bazaar. Gold is usually priced by weight plus a making charge, so ask for both numbers separately and compare a few shops before you commit.

Fabric: if you want material by the metre for tailoring, the bazaar's fabric sellers have far more range than any mall.

Furniture and bulk groceries: people drive out to the larger stores on the edges of the city, where there is room for big showrooms, wholesale-style shopping and easier parking.

Salim Street is still worth it for branded items, a shop you already trust, or just the experience.

A few practical tips:
- Carry Iraqi dinars in cash. Smaller bazaar shops often prefer it.
- Ask a local friend for a ballpark price before buying anything expensive.
- In the bazaar, the first price is rarely final, and polite bargaining is normal.
