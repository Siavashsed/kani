## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What kinds of startups are actually succeeding in Kurdistan, Iraq?

The startups gaining real traction in the Kurdistan Region are mostly not in oil or real estate, which is what people tend to assume. They cluster in four areas: payments, delivery, logistics, and software that sells outside the Region.

The main reason is the local economy's cash flow. Many households and businesses depend on public salaries, and when those payments arrive late or irregularly, any business that relies on steady local spending gets squeezed. Founders learn quickly to build around that risk.

Payments and delivery work because they fix problems people already face every day. Cash handling is clumsy, courier coverage is uneven, and informal workarounds are everywhere. A startup that makes these smoother can charge small fees on high volumes of transactions, which holds up better than a one-off big-ticket sale.

Logistics follows the same logic. Erbil, Sulaymaniyah, and Duhok are connected to trade routes into the rest of Iraq, Turkey, and Iran, and moving goods reliably is valuable.

Software exported abroad is the fourth category. Teams that sell to customers in Europe, the Gulf, or North America earn in stronger currencies and aren't tied to the local payroll cycle. Remote work and cheaper cloud tools have made this easier than it was a decade ago.

The common thread is that successful founders pick problems with daily demand or foreign revenue, and they're comfortable stitching together imperfect infrastructure rather than waiting for it to improve. The biggest obstacles remain banking access, licensing friction, and limited local venture funding.
