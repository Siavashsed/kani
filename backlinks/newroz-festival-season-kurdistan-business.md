I spent last March 20 walking around the Erbil Citadel area early in the morning, and the gap between neighboring businesses was striking. Newroz seems to be the best week of the year for some businesses and the worst for others. ## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How does Newroz affect businesses in Kurdistan, and how should owners prepare?

Newroz, celebrated around March 20-21, is the Kurdish new year and one of the biggest public holidays in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq. Its effect on business is sharply uneven, and the gap between winners and losers usually comes down to preparation.

Who benefits: Hotels, restaurants, cafes, bakeries, sweets makers, transport services, and anyone selling picnic supplies, traditional clothing or festival goods typically see their strongest demand of the year. Families travel to Erbil (Hewler), Sulaymaniyah (Slemani), Duhok and mountain areas, so hospitality and food businesses can fill up and sell out early. On one Newroz morning near the Erbil Citadel, a bakery had sold roughly 300 pieces of kleicha by 7:40 am.

Who loses out: Retail shops, offices, electronics stores and many services can go almost silent. Customers are outdoors, staff want extended time off, and the city centre empties. One shop owner told us he lost about three days of sales because he treated the holiday like a normal week.

How to prepare:
1. Decide early whether you will close, extend hours or change your offer, and tell staff and customers in advance.
2. If you are in food or hospitality, order stock and book staff weeks ahead, and plan for early-morning peaks.
3. If you are in retail or services, consider holiday promotions, online orders, or a planned closure that controls costs instead of an accidental one.
4. Look at last year's sales by day, not just by week, to see exactly when the surge and the slump hit.
5. Keep cash flow in mind: fixed costs like rent and salaries continue even when sales stop.

The core lesson is that Newroz is predictable. It lands on the same date every year, so there is no excuse for being surprised.
