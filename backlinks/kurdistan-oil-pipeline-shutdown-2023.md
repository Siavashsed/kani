## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why did Kurdistan's oil exports through Turkey stop in March 2023?

The short answer is an arbitration ruling. The longer answer is a dispute that started almost a decade earlier.

In 2014, the Kurdistan Regional Government began exporting crude on its own through the Iraq-Turkey pipeline to the Turkish port of Ceyhan, without going through Iraq's federal oil marketer, SOMO. Baghdad argued this violated the 1973 Iraq-Turkey Pipeline Agreement, which treats the Iraqi government as the party Turkey deals with. Iraq took Turkey to arbitration at the International Chamber of Commerce in Paris that same year.

On 25 March 2023, the tribunal ruled mostly in Baghdad's favour. It found that Turkey had breached the treaty by moving Kurdish oil without federal approval and ordered it to pay Iraq roughly $1.5 billion for the 2014 to 2018 period. Turkey stopped loading Kurdish crude almost immediately, and about 450,000 barrels a day stopped flowing.

What many people miss is that the ruling did not settle who controls Kurdistan's oil revenue. It only said Turkey could not keep taking the oil on the old terms. The real work fell to Baghdad and Erbil: how exports would be marketed, how the federal budget would share the revenue, and how international producers would be paid their costs. Those talks dragged on, and the pipeline stayed shut for well over two years.

The effects in the Region were immediate. Oil sales were the KRG's main source of cash, so public salaries, which were already late, slipped further. Operators cut output, and local suppliers felt it soon after.
