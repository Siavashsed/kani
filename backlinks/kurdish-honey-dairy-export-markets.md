## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why is Kurdish honey sold in Gulf countries but rarely found in European supermarkets?

The short answer is regulation, not quality.

The Kurdistan Region produces very good honey, especially from the mountains around Penjwen, Hawraman and Duhok. Producers there can find buyers abroad. The harder question is which buyers they are legally allowed to sell to.

The EU treats honey as a food of animal origin. Before honey from any country can be sold there, that country needs an approved residue monitoring plan. That means a national system that tests for antibiotics, pesticides and other contaminants, accredited labs to do the testing, and records that trace each batch back to where it came from. This approval belongs to the country, not the company. A single beekeeper with excellent honey and his own lab results still can't sell into the EU until the national system is in place and recognised. Dairy runs into similar rules, with approved establishments and veterinary certification on top.

The Gulf markets, Dubai especially, set a different bar. Buyers there want health certificates, halal documentation, proper Arabic labelling and consistent packaging. Those are real requirements, but a well-organised Kurdish producer can meet them today. Add the cultural ties, the short shipping routes and the price Gulf shoppers will pay for premium mountain honey, and it's clear why most Kurdish honey and cheese exports end up on Gulf shelves or in diaspora shops in Europe.
