**Title:** Has anyone else noticed the Nawroz economy peaks weeks before March 21, not on the day?

Something I've noticed around Erbil that doesn't get talked about much: most Nawroz spending happens before the holiday, not during it.

In March 2022 I went to a tailor off the Qaysari bazaar on the 10th to get a kawa w sharwal made. He laughed and opened a notebook with 63 orders ahead of mine. I ended up wearing my cousin's old suit.

The same thing happens every year. Tailors, fabric sellers, sweet shops, butchers and grocers do most of their Nawroz business in the three weeks before March 21. Families buy clothes, stock up for picnics and get gifts sorted. Then the holiday comes and the city half empties as everyone heads for the mountains, Shaqlawa, Soran and the picnic spots along the roads. Plenty of shops in town just close.

If you run a business here, or you're visiting, the timing matters more than people think. Get clothes ordered in February. On the day itself, the crowds are in the mountains, not the bazaar.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How does Nawroz affect tourism and local business in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq?

Nawroz has a real effect on tourism and local trade in the Kurdistan Region, but the timing surprises people. Most of the money moves in the three weeks before March 21, not on the holiday itself.

Before the holiday: This is the peak for anyone selling clothing, food or gifts. Traditional dress is the clearest example. Tailors around Erbil's Qaysari bazaar take orders for kawa w sharwal weeks in advance, and by mid-March many are fully booked. One of our team walked into a tailor's shop on March 10, 2022 and was shown a notebook with 63 orders already waiting. Fabric sellers, sweet makers, butchers, grocers and shops selling picnic gear all get the same early rush.

During the holiday: Erbil and Sulaymaniyah get noticeably quieter. Families leave for the mountains, rivers and picnic areas, and many city shops close or shorten their hours. Spending moves to fuel, roadside food, and resort towns such as Shaqlawa and Soran. Hotels and guesthouses there fill up, and visitors from elsewhere in Iraq add to the traffic.

After the holiday: City trade picks back up gradually as people return.

What this means in practice:
- If you want traditional clothing made, order it in February.
- If you run a city business, your Nawroz season is early March, so stock and staff for that.
- If you're visiting, the festive atmosphere is outdoors, around the bonfires and picnic sites, not in the bazaars.
