## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why does Kurdistan export raw agricultural products instead of branded food, and how can local brands capture more value?

Short version: Kurdistan has the farmland, but much of the value is created after the farm gate, and that is where most of it is lost.

The Region's soil, water and climate support serious wheat, barley, fruit, vegetables and honey production. The problem is what happens next. A lot of produce leaves raw, in bulk and unlabelled. Once it reaches an importer, they add the packaging, the brand and the story, and they keep the margin.

A small example: mountain honey from Surdash sells for about 18,000 dinars in a Slemani shop. Relabelled by a Gulf importer, the same honey can sit on a Dubai shelf at roughly four times that price. The product did not change. The branding and trust did.

Why does this happen?

1. Processing capacity is thin. Without mills, packing lines and cold storage nearby, raw export is the easy option.
2. Certification and testing are hard to access for small producers, and export buyers expect them.
3. Branding is treated as a luxury rather than part of the product.
4. Financing is limited for working capital, equipment and export paperwork.
5. Fragmented producers rarely have the scale to negotiate with large importers.

How local brands can capture more value:

- Pool smaller producers into cooperatives with a shared brand and quality standard.
- Invest in packaging, labelling and clear provenance, such as the village, altitude and harvest season.
- Build relationships with retailers and distributors directly instead of selling through a single middleman.
- Start with high-margin, shelf-stable goods like honey, dried fruit, nuts and spices before moving to harder categories.

None of this requires new land, only better systems around the land the Region already has.
