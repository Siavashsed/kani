The Top 12 Things to Do in Erbil

Twelve essential experiences in Erbil, from the UNESCO Citadel and Qaysari Bazaar to Sami Abdulrahman Park, the malls, and mountain day trips.

Erbil, known to Kurds as Hewler, is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities on earth, and that depth of history sits comfortably alongside gleaming modern towers, sprawling parks, and lively malls. A visit here can move from the ancient stone of a UNESCO World Heritage citadel to the buzz of a covered bazaar, from green public spaces built for families to mountain roads leading toward cool, scenic getaways. The city is welcoming, walkable in its center, and increasingly geared toward visitors. Whether you have a single packed day or a relaxed week, these twelve experiences capture the best of what Erbil offers, blending its proud heritage with its energetic present.

1. The Erbil Citadel

Rising on a tell at the heart of the city, the Erbil Citadel is the unmissable centerpiece of any visit. Inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list, this fortified mound is widely cited as one of the oldest continuously inhabited sites in the world, its layered history reaching back thousands of years. Walking up to its gate and through its old lanes is to step directly into the past.

Inside you will find restored houses, museums including a noted textile museum, and sweeping views over the modern city below. Take your time, learn the layers of its story, and return after dark when the walls are beautifully floodlit above the square.

2. Qaysari Bazaar

Spilling out from the base of the Citadel, the Qaysari Bazaar is a labyrinth of covered lanes that has been the commercial soul of Erbil for centuries. Here you can lose yourself among stalls selling spices, textiles, tea, household goods, and glittering gold, all to a soundtrack of bargaining and clinking tea glasses.

It is an experience for all the senses and the best place to feel the everyday rhythm of the city. Bargaining is part of the culture, so engage with good humor, pause for tea in a tucked-away chaikhana, and let yourself wander without a fixed plan.

3. Shar Park and the Citadel Square

The grand public square and Shar Park spread out directly below the Citadel, forming the lively, beating heart of central Erbil. With its large fountain, open plazas, and constant flow of families, vendors, and visitors, it is where the city comes to relax, especially in the cooler evenings.

Surrounded by cafes and offering a perfect vantage of the illuminated Citadel walls, the square is ideal for people-watching and soaking up the atmosphere. It costs nothing and rewards you simply for showing up and sitting a while.

4. Sami Abdulrahman Park

Erbil's largest and most beloved green space, Sami Abdulrahman Park is a vast expanse of lawns, lakes, walking paths, and shaded picnic areas. Once a military base, it was transformed into a public park and has become a symbol of the city's renewal and a daily refuge for residents.

Come to jog or stroll along the water, rent a paddle boat, picnic with a family, or simply escape the urban bustle. In the evenings and on weekends it fills with locals, offering one of the best windows into how the city relaxes and recreates.

5. Shanidar Park

Centrally located and easy to reach, Shanidar Park is a popular landscaped park dotted with greenery, water features, and play areas. It draws families and couples, especially in the late afternoon and evening, and its manicured grounds make for a pleasant, low-key outing.

The park is a fine place to slow down between sightseeing, let children play, and enjoy a casual walk. Its accessibility and relaxed atmosphere make it a reliable choice when you want a green break without venturing far from the center.

6. Minare Park and the Mudhafaria Minaret

Minare Park takes its name from the historic Mudhafaria Minaret, a striking brick minaret dating back roughly eight centuries and one of the city's most important surviving medieval monuments. The park around it combines this heritage landmark with gardens, walkways, and recreational space.

It is a rewarding stop for those interested in the region's Islamic architectural history, and the leafy surroundings make it pleasant for a walk. A cable car has at times operated here, linking the park area and adding a fun vantage over the greenery for visitors.

7. Jalil Khayat Mosque

Often described as one of the most beautiful mosques in the Kurdistan Region, the Jalil Khayat Mosque is a graceful, modern structure whose design draws inspiration from celebrated mosques of the wider Islamic world. Its domes, minarets, and intricate interior detailing make it a genuine architectural highlight.

Visitors are generally welcomed with appropriate, modest dress and respect for prayer times, and the serene interior is worth seeing. Even admired from outside, especially when lit in the evening, it is one of the city's most photogenic landmarks.

8. Family Mall and Majidi Mall

For modern leisure, shopping, and a reprieve from the summer heat, Erbil's major malls deliver. Family Mall, anchored by international retailers and home to attractions like an ice rink and a large hypermarket, and Majidi Mall, another popular shopping and dining destination, are both social hubs as much as retail centers.

Expect cinemas, food courts, cafes, supermarkets, and entertainment for children, all under one roof. They are especially valuable for families and for cooling off on hot days, and they offer a window into contemporary, consumer-facing Erbil.

9. Kurdish Textile and Cultural Museums

To deepen your understanding of Kurdish heritage, seek out the museums within and around the Citadel, including the well-regarded textile museum that showcases traditional rugs, kilims, costumes, and crafts. These collections illuminate the artistry, daily life, and identity of the Kurdish people.

The exhibits are thoughtfully presented and add meaningful context to everything else you see in the city. For culturally curious travelers, an hour or two here transforms a sightseeing trip into a real appreciation of the region's living traditions.

10. Sample the Local Cuisine

Eating is itself a top thing to do in Erbil. Make a point of trying the regional staples: charcoal kebab, slow-grilled masgouf fish, stuffed dolma, fragrant biryani, fresh bread straight from the oven, and glass after glass of sweet tea. The variety, from Kurdish home cooking to Lebanese, Turkish, and Iranian kitchens, is part of the pleasure.

Wander the dining streets of the Italian Village, the garden restaurants of Ankawa, or a humble family grill, and you will taste the city's generosity firsthand. Food here is social and abundant, so come hungry and plan to share.

11. Day Trip to Shaqlawa

When the city heat builds, locals head for the hills, and Shaqlawa is among the most popular escapes. This mountain resort town, set amid orchards and greenery a scenic drive north of Erbil, offers cooler air, fresh fruit and honey stalls, walking trails, and a relaxed holiday mood.

It makes an easy and rewarding day trip, with winding roads opening onto beautiful landscapes along the way. Spend the day strolling the bazaar, sampling local produce, and enjoying the mountain views before returning to the city in the evening.

12. Day Trip toward Salahaddin and the Mountains

The resort town of Salahaddin, perched on high ground not far from Erbil, is another favored mountain getaway, known for its elevated position, cooler climate, and panoramic views over the surrounding country. The drive itself, climbing into the hills, is part of the appeal.

Combined with nearby scenic spots and villages, a trip in this direction showcases the natural beauty that sits just beyond the city. For visitors with an extra day, escaping into the mountains offers a refreshing contrast to Erbil's urban core and a glimpse of the wider Kurdistan landscape.

Erbil rewards travelers who let it unfold at its own pace: a morning climbing into the ancient Citadel, an afternoon bargaining in the bazaar and resting in a green park, an evening feast, and perhaps a day escaping into the cool of the mountains. Few cities pack so much history, hospitality, and modern comfort into such a manageable footprint. Take your time, talk to people, accept the tea, and you will leave understanding why Kurds speak of Hewler with such pride.
